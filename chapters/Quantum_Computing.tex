\chapter{Quantum computation} \label{chap:Quantum_computation}
The main idea for using Majorana fermions for quantum computation is through a process called braiding. In this process the Majoranas are spatially rotated around eachother, producing a difference in phase. In 1D this is however hard to do: If two Majoranas meet, they simply combine into a normal fermion, allowing the stored information to decohere. Several methods are being discussed to overcome this obstacle, and we will closer study especially one of them; the T-junction. In this junction we will show that one can perform the Pauli X and Z gates (and therefore also Y) but we still need an extra gate, not provided by topologically protected operations, in order to create a general quantum computer.

"A topological quantum computer is constructed using a system in a non-Abelian topological phase. A computation is
performed by creating quasiparticles, braiding them, and measuring their final state. " \cite{nayakNonAbelianAnyonsTopological2008}

\subsection{Degenerate ground states} \label{subsec:Degenerate_ground_states}
Recalling the previously discussed Kitaev chain Hamiltonian, and the following definition using the Majorana operators $c_{m}$ with properties 
\begin{align}
    d_j &= \frac{1}{2} \left(c_{2j} + i c_{2j+1} \right) \\
    d_j^\dagger &= \frac{1}{2} \left(c_{2j} - i c_{2j+1} \right)\\
    c_{m}^\dagger &= c_m, \hspace{4mm} \{c_l, c_m\} = 2\delta_{lm}.
\end{align}
We recall that in the trivial phase, Majoranas on all sites pair up into ordinary fermions with non-zero energy. However in the topological phase the chain experiences unpaired Majoranas at the edges, which we want well-separated (meaning their wavefunctions overlap negligibly). For a 1D wire of $2M$ such edge Majoranas one can generally construct $M$ fermion operators $f_{lm}$ \footnote{Note here that $f_{lm}$ is a special case of the the general $d_j$ shown above, only $f_{lm}$ is specifically for the Majorana edge modes} with the properties
\begin{align}
    f_{lm} &= \frac{1}{2} \left(c_{l} + i c_{m} \right) \\
    \{f_{lm}, f^\dagger_{lm}\} &= 1 \\
    f_{ij}^2 &= \left(f_{ij}^\dagger\right)^2 = 0
\end{align}
These correspond to zero-energy modes, and can give rise to $2^M$ degenerate ground states which can be labeled by occupation numbers $n_j = f_j^\dagger f_j = 0, 1$.

\subsection{T-junction}
The T-junction is a proposed architecture for allowing braiding with a 1D Kitaev chain. Simply put, the idea stems from connecting a 1D chain to the middle of another, producing a T-shape. In this geometry, one can in theory braid the Majoranas by altering the parameters of the Hamiltonian to rotate the edge states around eachother without touching.
